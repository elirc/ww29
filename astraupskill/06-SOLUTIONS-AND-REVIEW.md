# Separate solutions and review rubric

Level 1 returns 404 and performs no mutation because the viewer lacks any-recipient visibility and the notification is treated as unavailable. Level 2 returns 409 because no delivery is SENT; no status refresh or audit should run. Level 3 marks the sent delivery opened, writes the notification timestamp, derives COMPLETED notification and batch statuses in the fixture, and audits the opened delivery id. Level 4 changes only the recipient visibility decision; the service still requires SENT and performs the same transition. Level 5 requires an explicit idempotency policy and provider-backed test; the current code's behavior should not be guessed from the lab.

Review on ownership, lifecycle ordering, aggregate refresh, and audit fidelity. The six-case lab executes the production service and permission matrix, so it earns service contract credit. It does not prove route validation, Prisma transaction rollback, worker delivery, or repeated-request policy. Reject tests that only call `markDeliveryOpened` or copy the role predicate into the test. Ask whether status derivation treats SKIPPED as completed according to product policy and whether opening multiple deliveries is intentionally supported. A strong review names the exact layer responsible for each decision and keeps privacy errors indistinguishable from not-found responses.
When status refresh fails, delivery and timestamp calls have already completed; audit is not reached. The source offers no enclosing transaction to undo those calls. A future change must choose transactional persistence or explicit reconciliation. Mixed OPENED and FAILED statuses yield PARTIAL_FAILURE under the actual helper.
